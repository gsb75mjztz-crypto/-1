# InvestorHub — Validation Sprint: Do Active Beginner Investors Actually Need This?

**Budget target:** £0–50. **Timeline target:** 2–3 weeks, run in parallel, before any product code gets written.

The whole point of this sprint is to be willing to find out the answer is no — every method below is designed to surface disconfirming evidence, not to collect polite encouragement. Optimism is the thing most likely to kill this validation's usefulness.

**Governing rule for all four methods (the "Mom Test" principle):** never ask what someone would do hypothetically. Ask what they have done. "Would you use a tool like this?" gets a polite yes from almost everyone regardless of real intent. "Tell me about the last time you picked between two ETFs — walk me through exactly what you did" gets the truth.

## 1. Interview Questions (20 people, ~15 minutes each)

**Screening** (before you book the call): must currently hold at least one ETF/stock, contribute at least occasionally, age roughly 18–26. Find them where they already are — replies on finance TikTok/X posts, a Reddit investing thread, your own social circle, Discord investing servers. Don't recruit from a place that pre-selects for high sophistication (e.g. a hardcore FIRE forum) — that's not the target cohort.

**Opening (2 min)** — get specifics, not vibes:

- "What do you currently hold, roughly, and how did you end up choosing those?"
- "How often do you add money, and what usually triggers it — payday, a specific decision, just habit?"

**Core — past behaviour on the actual decision this product targets (8–10 min):**

- "Tell me about the last time you picked between two similar funds, or added a new position — walk me through exactly what happened, step by step." (This single question is the most important one in the whole script — let them talk, don't lead.)
- "What did you actually look at or read before deciding? Show me if you can — pull up your phone."
- "Was there a moment you felt unsure or second-guessed yourself? What did you do about it — did you look something up, ask someone, or just go with your gut?"
- "Who or what do you trust most for this kind of decision, and why them specifically?"
- "Has anything gone wrong, or have you found out later you wish you'd known something sooner? What was it?"
- "What's the last thing you looked up about investing — not generally, the literal last thing — and where did you look it up?"

**Closing — gentle forward-look, kept honest (2–3 min):**

- "If a tool had shown you [describe the comparison tool/calculator in one sentence, concretely] at that exact moment — would that have changed anything, or were you already confident?" (Expect most people to say it would help — treat this answer skeptically; it's the one hypothetical question in the script, included only to get colour, not as evidence on its own.)
- "Is there anyone else you know who's been through something similar recently, that I could talk to?" (Referral chain — cheapest way to hit 20 interviews fast.)

**What counts as a real signal vs. a false positive:**

- Real signal: unprompted frustration, a specific workaround they built themselves (a spreadsheet, a group chat they ask), a concrete story of a costly mistake or hesitation.
- False positive: general agreement that "investing is confusing" (almost universally true and tells you nothing specific), or enthusiasm only appearing once you've described the product back to them.

## 2. Survey (quantitative layer, distributed for free)

Keep it under 2 minutes to complete — 6–8 questions max, single distribution via X/Reddit/Discord/your own network, no incentive needed if kept this short.

1. Do you currently hold any ETFs or individual stocks? (Yes / No — screens respondents)
2. How did you decide what to invest in when you started? (open text — free-text answers here are worth more than any multiple-choice option, because the exact words people use are your future landing-page copy)
3. When did you last compare two specific funds or stocks before deciding? (Never / Within the last month / 1–6 months ago / Over 6 months ago)
4. What's the single most confusing part of investing for you right now? (open text)
5. Where do you currently go to check something before investing? (select all: Google, YouTube/TikTok, Reddit, a friend/family member, a broker app's own content, nowhere — I just decide, other: ___)
6. On a 1–5 scale, how confident are you that your current portfolio is well thought out? (low self-reported confidence + high activity = your strongest quantitative signal)
7. Would you be interested in trying a free tool for this? (Yes / No / Maybe) — include this last and weight it least; per the Mom Test principle, stated interest is the weakest data point in the whole survey, not the strongest. Use it only as a rough top-of-funnel filter, never as the headline result.

**Target:** 100+ responses is enough to see a real pattern in the open-text answers (Q2, Q4) — that's where the actual insight lives, far more than the scale questions.

## 3. Landing Page Test (fake door / smoke test)

**Build:** a single free/near-free page (Carrd, a simple Framer/Notion page, or a one-page static site) — not the product, a description of it. One sentence of positioning, a short explanation of the comparison-tool-plus-calculator concept, a single call to action.

The CTA is the actual experiment, and it needs to cost the visitor something — free page views with a vague "sign up for updates" box measure almost nothing, because it's frictionless to click. Better options, in order of signal strength:

- **Best:** "Run this comparison for your specific ETF" — a form asking them to type in two actual funds they're deciding between, promising a personal reply within 48 hours (which you then genuinely do manually — this doubles as more Mom Test interview material).
- **Good:** email capture gated behind something specific — "get notified when the fee-drag calculator launches," not a generic "join the waitlist."
- **Weak but acceptable as a secondary metric:** a simple waitlist email box.

**Traffic, free:** post the link directly into the same channels used for interview recruitment and the survey — your own X/TikTok if you're building an audience there already (Section 4 ties directly into this), relevant subreddits (checking each one's self-promotion rules first), Discord investing servers, a Reddit "I built this, feedback welcome" style post.

**Numbers that would actually indicate something** (rough benchmarks, not laws):

- Under 1% of visitors converting to the costed CTA → weak signal, the value proposition probably isn't landing or isn't urgent enough.
- 3–5%+ → worth taking seriously.
- Watch what people type into the "your specific funds" field even more closely than the conversion rate itself — that's real evidence of the actual decisions people are stuck on, and it's the single richest data source in this entire sprint.

## 4. X (Content) Validation

This tests the second, equally important assumption from the distribution strategy — not just "does the product idea land," but "can founder-led content on the actual channel reach the actual audience at all." If this fails, it invalidates the go-to-market plan regardless of what the interviews and landing page say.

**What to post** (5–10 pieces over 2–3 weeks, not one and done):

- A real, specific comparison you're personally making — "I'm choosing between VWRP and a split of individual regional ETFs, here's the actual fee-drag math over 20 years" — with a real number, not a general "fees matter" statement.
- A concrete "here's a mistake I nearly made" post — specific and honest performs far better than generic education content, and it's also more genuine.
- One post directly asking the validation question: "what's the most confusing part of picking an ETF for you?" — replies here are free interview leads.

**What to measure** — engagement quality over volume:

- Replies containing a specific question or their own number/fund name (real signal) vs. generic "great post" replies (noise — largely ignore these for validation purposes).
- Saves/bookmarks specifically, where the platform shows them — a strong "I want to come back to this" signal, often more meaningful than likes.
- Any unprompted DM or reply asking "is there a tool for this" or "can you check mine" — this is the single strongest piece of evidence the whole sprint can produce, because it's someone asking for the product before it exists, unprompted.

**Realistic go/no-go read on this channel specifically:** a handful of pieces getting genuine, specific engagement from people who look like the target cohort (check their bio/activity, not just follower count) is a real signal at this stage — don't wait for virality as the bar, since the point right now is validating reachability and resonance, not proving the whole growth strategy in three weeks.

## Pulling it together — what "validated" actually looks like

Don't score these independently — look for the same specific pain surfacing across at least three of the four methods. If interviews surface "I never know if I'm too concentrated in one sector" as an unprompted, specific, recurring frustration, and the survey's open-text answers echo similar language, and the landing page's "your specific funds" field shows people typing in genuinely overlapping sector-heavy portfolios, and X replies ask a version of the same question — that's real convergent evidence, worth building for.

A single method producing enthusiasm on its own, especially the survey's Q7 or a landing page's raw visitor count, is the weakest possible basis to start building on, and the Mom Test discipline throughout this sprint exists specifically to stop early enthusiasm from being mistaken for it.
